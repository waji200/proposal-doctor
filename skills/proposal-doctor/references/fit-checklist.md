# Fit checklist: does this proposal answer this client's problem?

Use it on any draft, with the job post open beside it. Score each line 0 (missing or wrong),
1 (partly) or 2 (clearly there). Each line has a test, so the score is something you can
point to, not a feeling.

**25 lines, 50 points. Ready to send at 40 or more, with no zero on the five lines marked
(must).** A high score is a quality bar, not a promise of a reply.

## A. Did you read it? (10)

| # | Check | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Names the client's real outcome, in their words | Underline the sentence that says what they get and what it is for. If it only names the task ("build a dashboard"), score 1. |
| 2 (must) | Uses two or more details from the post, each tied to a consequence | For each detail ask "so what?". "You have 14 apps" is an echo. "14 apps is where your score goes" is used. |
| 3 (must) | Every question answered, every requested item present, code word in place | Tick them off against the post one by one, including the last line of the description. |
| 4 | Matches the client's level and language | A founder who wrote "make it faster" should not get "LCP, CLS and hydration". A CTO who wrote a stack list should not get baby talk. |
| 5 | Nothing contradicts the post | Check stack, budget, deadline, tools they said they already use, and what they said they do not want. |

## B. Did you find the hard part? (6)

| # | Check | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Says what is hard or risky about this job | Is there a sentence a beginner could not have written? |
| 7 | Stays conditional where you have not seen the code or files | "Usually", "I'd check first" on diagnoses. No confident verdict on a codebase you have not opened. |
| 8 | Does not lecture, correct the client or invent problems | No "you should really be using...". No scare tactics. |

## C. Is there a solution? (10)

| # | Check | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Names the first concrete step | "I'd start by..." followed by an action, not a phase name like "discovery". |
| 10 | Names the first thing they receive, and when | A deliverable and a time: "the home page before/after score within a day". |
| 11 | States what the bid covers (fixed-price) | One line with the scope and the main assumption. Score 2 automatically for hourly jobs with no scope asked. |
| 12 | Works with their stack and constraints | Does not propose a rewrite, a different platform or a bigger project than asked. |
| 13 | Leaves something to talk about | Gives the first move and the reasoning, not a full plan in ten steps. |

## D. Is there proof? (10)

| # | Check | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 14 (must) | One closest proof, same problem first | Would the client say "that's like mine"? Same framework alone scores 1. |
| 15 | The proof has something checkable | A link, an attached item, a number or a review. A project name with no way to look at it scores 0. |
| 16 | Your part in it is clear and true | "I built the booking flow" beats "worked on". Never claim sole credit for team work. |
| 17 | No credential wall | Years, badges and tool lists take at most one clause, after the proof. |
| 18 | No apologies | A real gap is stated once, plainly, with what you would do about it. Repeated disclaimers score 0. A gap in the main requirement means the job should have been skipped. |

## E. Is it easy to say yes? (8)

| # | Check | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 19 (must) | Ends with one specific, easy question or next step | Could the client answer in one line from their phone? |
| 20 | Asks for no meeting as the first step, and has no "let me know" | The client can reply without booking anything. |
| 21 | Rate or bid fits their budget, or the gap is explained in a line | Compare with the range on the post. |
| 22 | Availability and timezone are given if asked | With a concrete overlap window, not "flexible". |

## F. Would it survive the pile? (6)

| # | Check | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | Swap test | Cover the job title. Count the sentences that could sit under a different job unchanged. More than a third: score 0. |
| 24 (must) | The preview alone earns the click | Read only the first 200 characters. Does this client recognise their own project? Is there a reason to read the next line? |
| 25 | The card backs the letter | Profile title and top skills match the job's main skill; the portfolio items attached are the ones the letter cites. |

## Reading the score

- **45 to 50**: send. Run the voice check first.
- **40 to 44**: fix the lowest two lines, then send.
- **30 to 39**: rewrite around the weakest section. Polishing sentences will not help.
- **Under 30, or a zero on a (must) line that cannot be fixed with true facts**: this is
  usually a job-gate problem, not a writing problem. Skip the job.

## The three failures that look like success

These pass a quick read and still lose.

1. **The expert essay.** Technically excellent, 300 words, a full plan. The client learns
   everything and has no reason to reply. Fix lines 13 and 19.
2. **The accurate echo.** Every requirement restated correctly, nothing added. It proves the
   post was processed, not understood. Fix lines 2 and 6.
3. **The honest apology.** True, careful, and full of sentences about what the freelancer
   has not done. Honesty is required; arguing against yourself is not. Fix line 18, or skip.
